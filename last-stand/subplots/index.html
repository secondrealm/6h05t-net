Subplot ideas

The source document brainstorms possible additions to the four-act outline. These are exploratory suggestions, not established plot events or completed character arcs.

The number of subplots a story can effectively handle varies based on factors such as the length of the book, the complexity of the main plot, and the writer's skill in juggling multiple storylines. That said, it's important to ensure that your subplots don't overwhelm your main plot.

For a typical novel, one to three subplots is often manageable and can add depth and richness to the narrative. More than that, and you risk diluting the impact of your main plot and confusing your readers. Remember, each subplot you introduce needs to be developed and resolved in a satisfying way, which takes narrative "space".

For longer or more complex novels, such as high fantasy or epic science fiction, you might have more subplots. These could involve a wide cast of characters and various interweaving storylines. Still, even in these cases, each subplot should be relevant to the main story in some way, either thematically, or by impacting the main plot or character development.

Ultimately, the number of subplots isn't as important as how well they're integrated into the story. If a subplot feels unnecessary or distracting, it may be best to remove it, regardless of how well they're integrated into the story. Good subplots enrich the main story, provide deeper insight into characters or the story world, and maintain the reader's interest throughout the book.

  1. Rebel Within the Remnants: There could be a character within the Remnants who begins to question the group's mission and their own humanity. This character's internal struggle and eventual decision to help the Sentinels could add another layer to the story.
  2. Sentinel Betrayal Aftermath: The aftermath of Sam's betrayal could lead to a subplot involving trust and unity within the Sentinels. This could involve one or more members struggling with feelings of guilt or suspicion, which they must overcome to function as a team again.
  3. The Origins of 6h05t.net: A backstory revealing the creation and early days of the 6h05t.net could serve as a subplot, offering a deeper understanding of its importance and why the Sentinels fight so hard to defend it.
  4. Alex's Past: Alex's backstory could be explored, perhaps revealing a past encounter with the Remnants or a personal loss that motivates his fight. This could add depth to his character and provide additional emotional stakes.
  5. Zoe's Captivity: During her time as a captive of the Remnants, Zoe could be involved in a subplot where she learns crucial information about the Remnants' plans or weaknesses. This could involve forming a dangerous alliance with a member of the Remnants or a daring escape attempt.
  6. Romantic Tension: The romantic tension between Alex and Zoe could be developed as a subplot, with both characters dealing with their feelings amid the ongoing conflict. This could involve moments of doubt, jealousy, or misunderstanding that they must resolve.
  7. Remnants' Leader Conflict: The leader of the Remnants could be grappling with maintaining control over the increasingly independent hybrids. This power struggle could add a layer of internal conflict within the antagonist's ranks.
  8. The Resistance Movement: There might be a subplot involving ordinary citizens starting a resistance movement against the Remnants, inspired by the Sentinels' actions.
  9. The Engineer: A subplot could follow an engineer or scientist who originally contributed to the technology that led to the Remnants' creation, now filled with regret and seeking redemption by secretly aiding the Sentinels.
  10. The Spy: A Sentinel could be working undercover in the Remnants, feeding crucial information back to the Sentinels, but with their loyalty being tested as they become sympathetic to the Remnants' cause.
  11. Family Ties: One of the Sentinels could discover a loved one has chosen to become a Remnant, leading to a personal crisis and questioning of their mission.
  12. A New Recruit: A subplot could follow a new recruit's journey within the Sentinels, providing a fresh perspective on the group and the struggles they face. Their training and growth could parallel the main plot's progression.
  13. The Outside World: It could be interesting to explore a subplot that shows how the rest of the world is coping. Are there other groups fighting against the Remnants, or is there a safe haven where people live in fear of the day the Remnants will come for them?
  14. The Traitor's Redemption: Sam, the Sentinel who betrayed the group, could have a subplot where he regrets his decisions and works toward redemption, possibly even sacrificing himself for the group in a pivotal moment.
  15. The Remnants' Dissension: A faction within the Remnants might start to question the path they're on, leading to internal conflicts and potential alliances with the Sentinels.
  16. Forgotten Origins: A subplot could revolve around the discovery of old, forgotten technology or knowledge that could tip the balance of power, sparking a race between the Sentinels and the Remnants to uncover it first.
  17. Personal Vendetta: A member of the Sentinels could have a personal vendetta against the Remnants, perhaps because they lost a loved one to them. This could add an element of revenge to the plot.
  18. The Double Agent: A character believed to be a loyal member of the Remnants could be revealed as a double agent working for the Sentinels, adding an element of espionage and intrigue.
  19. The Prodigal Sentinel: A former Sentinel who left the group due to ideological differences could return, causing tension and conflict within the group, but ultimately contributing to their cause in a significant way.
  20. A Hidden Society: The Sentinels could discover a hidden society of humans who have managed to avoid detection by the Remnants. This society could provide unexpected aid, or could have their own agenda, complicating the Sentinels' mission.

Remember, subplots should enhance and deepen the main plot. They can offer opportunities for character development, increase the story's tension and stakes, and add complexity to your novel's world.
